Children's Dentistry

The American Dental Association recommends a first dental visit by age 1, or within 6 months of the first tooth appearing. Early visits help identify development issues, establish good oral hygiene habits, and get children comfortable with the dental setting. First visits at BrightSmile are always complimentary.

Talk about the visit positively and avoid using words like "hurt," "shot," or "drill." Read books about dental visits, play pretend dentist at home, and let them bring a favourite toy. Dr. Okonkwo is specially trained in paediatric behaviour guidance and will go at your child's pace — tours of the equipment, tell-show-do, and lots of encouragement are part of every visit.

Dental Emergencies

Dental emergencies include: severe toothache, knocked-out tooth, cracked or broken tooth, lost crown or filling, dental abscess (swelling with fever or pus), soft tissue injuries, and jaw injuries. If in doubt, call us — we'd rather hear from you and determine it's not urgent than have you suffer needlessly.

Aftercare Instructions

Follow these guidelines after your procedure for the best recovery. Call us at (555) 123-4567 if you have concerns.

After a Filling

  1. Avoid eating on the treated side for 2 hours
  2. Numbness may last 2–4 hours — avoid hot foods and drinks until it subsides
  3. Some sensitivity to cold, heat, or pressure is normal for 1–2 weeks
  4. Take over-the-counter pain relief (ibuprofen/paracetamol) if needed
  5. If your bite feels uneven, call us for a quick free adjustment
  6. Brush and floss normally — the filling is hard immediately

After a Tooth Extraction

  1. Bite firmly on the gauze for 30–60 minutes to form a clot
  2. Do not rinse, spit, or use straws for 24 hours — this can dislodge the clot
  3. Apply ice packs (20 min on, 20 min off) for the first 24 hours
  4. Eat soft foods only — soup, yoghurt, mashed potatoes — for 2–3 days
  5. Take prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication as directed
  6. No smoking, alcohol, or vigorous exercise for 48 hours
  7. Gently rinse with warm salt water (1/4 tsp salt per cup) starting 24 hours after

After Teeth Whitening

  1. Avoid staining foods and drinks for 48 hours: coffee, tea, red wine, berries, soy sauce
  2. No smoking for at least 48 hours
  3. Tooth sensitivity is normal for 24–48 hours — use sensitive toothpaste
  4. You may use a straw for cold drinks to minimise contact with teeth
  5. Stick to "white foods": chicken, fish, pasta, bananas, yoghurt for 2 days
  6. Maintain results with whitening toothpaste and your take-home trays

After a Root Canal

  1. Avoid chewing on the treated tooth until the permanent crown is placed
  2. Take antibiotics if prescribed — complete the full course
  3. Pain and swelling typically subside within 2–3 days
  4. Take ibuprofen with food every 6–8 hours for the first 2 days
  5. Mild tenderness when biting is normal for 1–2 weeks
  6. Book your crown appointment within 2–4 weeks to protect the tooth

New Invisalign Aligners

  1. Wear aligners 20–22 hours per day — remove only to eat and brush
  2. Some pressure and discomfort is normal for the first 2–3 days of each new tray
  3. Rinse aligners with cold water and clean with a soft toothbrush — no hot water
  4. Brush and floss before reinserting aligners to prevent staining and decay
  5. Store aligners in their case when not wearing — never in a napkin
  6. Carry your previous set as a backup — contact us immediately if aligners are lost

After Implant Surgery

  1. Bite on gauze for 45 minutes; change if still bleeding after 1 hour
  2. Apply ice packs for first 24 hours; swelling may peak at 48–72 hours
  3. Take all prescribed medications on schedule
  4. Eat very soft foods (yoghurt, smoothies, soup) for the first week
  5. Do not brush the implant site for 24 hours — gently rinse with saltwater
  6. No smoking for at least 2 weeks — smoking significantly impairs healing
  7. Attend all follow-up appointments for bone integration monitoring

Dental Education

Knowledge is your best defence against dental disease. Browse our patient education articles written by our clinical team.

Hygiene

The Right Way to Brush Your Teeth

Most people brush too hard or too fast. Learn the Modified Bass technique recommended by dental professionals — 2 minutes, 45° angle, gentle circular strokes.

Key takeaways:

  • Brush for a full 2 minutes, twice daily
  • Use a soft-bristled brush — medium/hard brushes damage enamel
  • Replace your brush every 3 months or after illness
  • Electric toothbrushes remove up to 21% more plaque
Hygiene

Why Flossing Matters More Than You Think

40% of your tooth surface is between teeth — completely unreachable by a toothbrush. Discover why flossing is non-negotiable and how to do it properly.

Key takeaways:

  • Floss once daily, ideally before bed
  • Curve floss into a C-shape around each tooth
  • Water flossers are a great alternative for braces or implants
  • Bleeding gums usually improve after 1–2 weeks of consistent flossing
Nutrition

Foods That Harm — and Help — Your Teeth

Your diet is one of the biggest factors in your dental health. Learn which foods to limit and which ones actually strengthen your teeth and gums.

Key takeaways:

  • Sugary and acidic foods fuel cavity-causing bacteria
  • Cheese, nuts, and leafy greens actively protect teeth
  • Wait 30 minutes after acidic foods before brushing
  • Drink water after meals to neutralise acids
Children

Your Child's Dental Development: A Timeline

From the first tooth at 6 months to the wisdom teeth in the late teens — understand what's normal at every stage and when to seek guidance.

Key takeaways:

  • Baby teeth matter — they hold space for permanent teeth
  • First dental visit recommended by age 1
  • All 20 baby teeth usually present by age 3
  • Orthodontic evaluation recommended around age 7
Gum Health

Understanding Gum Disease: Signs, Stages & Prevention

Gum disease is the leading cause of adult tooth loss — and it's largely preventable. Recognise the warning signs before they become irreversible.

Key takeaways:

  • Bleeding gums are a warning, not normal
  • Gingivitis is reversible; periodontitis is not
  • Gum disease is linked to heart disease and diabetes
  • Professional cleaning every 6 months prevents most gum disease
